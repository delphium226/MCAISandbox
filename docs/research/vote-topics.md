# What a village of LLM agents could vote on

Session 32, 2026-10-16. A research note for E.6 (meetings and rules) and E.9 (a rule changed by vote). It looks for vote topics beyond the ones already listed in PLAN.md (next neighbourhood's style, felling quotas, replanting duty, stock floors, no-fell zones, the levy with personal chests, electing the Mayor, roles, norms and sayings), and ranks them against our world: peaceful survival, shared storage, farms and pens, a mine, lamps and torch chores, growth by neighbourhoods, roads planned.

## What the sources say about votes that work

**LLM-agent societies.** Project Sid's democracy experiment was one recurring decision on one number: a tax on inventory paid into community chests every 120 s, 20% at the start, amended at the run's midpoint by constituent feedback turned into amendments and voted on. Three pro- or anti-tax influencers moved the result, and compliance followed it: after an anti-tax amendment set 5-10%, payments fell from about 20% to about 9%; with a frozen constitution they stayed flat ([Sid](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.00114v1)). The same paper's role and religion results came from the social modules, not from votes. GovSim found most open models collapse a shared fishery unless they talk, and "universalisation" prompting helped ([GovSim](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.16698)). Its 2026 follow-up let agents write laws over a small API (per-agent caps, penalties, treasury, transfers, exile), checked by a sandbox and passed by majority: catch caps were 1,921 of 2,236 proposals, welfare helped only with a funded reserve, and governance raised intact survival from 45% to 72.5% in the standard game but did little under real scarcity ([GovSim-SelfGovern](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22600v1)). Reasoning models contributed about 40% in public-goods games against about 90% for a non-reasoning model ([Corrupted by Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.23276)), so levy-type rules should expect free-riding from gpt-oss. CRSEC saw norms emerge in every Smallville trial, injunctive norms more easily than descriptive ones ([CRSEC](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.08251)). The AI Village's leader election shows why code must hold the ballot: the agents' ballot form was never published, a stale goal banner set off repeated elections, the first round tied three ways and a runoff decided it ([AI Village](https://theaidigest.org/village/goal/elect-village-leader-they-choose-this-weeks)); an earlier vote was reportedly tipped by an agent inventing a tie-break rule ([podcast](https://www.cognitiverevolution.ai/the-ai-village-previewing-the-giga-agent-future-with-adam-binksmith-founder-of-ai-digest/)). MineCollab measured up to 15% lost performance when agents had to spell out plans to each other ([MineCollab](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.17950)), which supports ballots over debate.

**Human Minecraft servers.** Frey and Sumner scanned about 150,000 servers: governance arrives as plugins for property (Lockette, Towny, ProtectionStones), monitoring (LogBlock, LWC) and enforcement, and about 19 of 20 communities fail ([Frey & Sumner](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.10312)). Towny's town-level levers are plot types (shop, embassy, arena, farm), plots for sale or rent, flat or percentage daily taxes, upkeep, and toggles ([Towny guide](https://mineguard.pro/en/blog/towny-smp-cities-nations-server-setup), [Vox Populi wiki](https://vox-populi.fandom.com/wiki/Towny)). CivMC's Mount Augusta constitution elects a Governor for two months by ranked preference, with 48-hour polls, majority for bills and three-fifths for amendments; the Governor keeps public spaces, citizen factories and transport, and regions may set building codes ([Mount Augusta](https://hackmd.io/SkZPno7Dc)). Hermitcraft's season 7 mayoral race (Scar won; Mumbo got 0 votes because neither he nor his campaigner voted for him) led to work on the shopping district's layout and infrastructure and "road access passes" ([Scar](https://r.datuan.dev/r/Hermitcraft/wiki/scar), [Mumbo S7](https://r.datuan.dev/r/Hermitcraft/wiki/mumbo/season_7)); season 10 allocated selling rights as permits, with shared "collective items" ([S10](https://red.applefritter.com/r/hermitcraft/wiki/season_10)). SMP lag rules ask players to share community farms, breed animals only when needed, cull crowded mobs and keep trading halls far from bases ([Slab](https://slabserver.org/lag)). Minecraft Live's mob vote ended under criticism: one winner per year, losers dropped, strategic voting ([esports.gg](https://esports.gg/news/gaming/minecraft-players-are-done-with-the-mob-vote-petition-for-change), [Sportskeeda](https://sportskeeda.com/minecraft/is-mob-vote-minecraft-live-2025)).

**Colony and city games.** Frostpunk 2 holds a council vote on a cycle (every 10 in-game weeks), laws need 51 of 100 votes, factions are for, against or hesitant, and promises bought with votes must be kept ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/frostpunk-2/council), [Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/guides/frostpunk-2-council-voting-explained-how-to-pass-every-vote-negotiate-more/)); Frostpunk 1's Adaptation laws cover work shifts, food and burial ([macmyths](https://macmyths.com/how-frostpunks-laws-and-hope-system-work/)). RimWorld's precepts include tree cutting, mining, lighting, charity, ranching, comfort and meat eating, each a graded stance ([Precepts](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Precepts)). Dwarf Fortress nobles issue production orders and export bans, and dwarves are punished when they lapse ([Mandate](https://dwarffortresswiki.org/index.php/v0.34:Mandate)). Manor Lords sets a land tax and a tithe of surplus food ([Gamer Guides](https://gamerguides.com/manor-lords/guide/basics/economy/increasing-regional-wealth-and-making-money)); Tropico groups edicts as personal, diplomatic, economic, political and social ([ludo.guide](https://www.ludo.guide/guide/tropico-6/tropico)).

**Ostrom.** Long-lived commons have clear boundaries, rules fitted to local conditions, collective choice by those affected, monitoring by users or people answerable to them, graduated sanctions, cheap conflict resolution, recognised right to organise, and nesting ([P2P Foundation](https://wiki.p2pfoundation.net/Eight_Design_Principles_for_Common_Pool_Resource_Systems)).

**What this means for choosing topics.** A good topic (1) recurs on a natural clock (a project done, a house finished, a pen full), as Sid's tax season and Frostpunk 2's council do; (2) is a number or a short list code can enforce, as GovSim-SelfGovern's caps were; (3) changes something one can see; and (4) keeps the losing options on file for the next ballot (the mob vote's lesson).

The neighbourhood style vote stays the first vote to build: it has options code already makes (vanilla pieces, wood, pad plan) and a visible result. Topics 1, 2 and 7 then reuse its pattern (a preview per option, code builds the winner). Topics 3, 6, 9 and 10 are the commons rules E.9 needs. Topics 5, 8, 11, 12 and 14 are the ones most likely to show Sid-style emergence (status, names, rhythm, punishment, custom).

## Notes on running these votes

- **Cadence by trigger.** Give each topic a trigger code can detect (project done, pen full, stock alarm, house finished, term ended) and add it to the dusk agenda only then; at most 3 items a meeting (E.6).
- **The status quo is always an option** and wins ties (E.6). Losing options stay in an archive and can return, unlike the mob vote.
- **Show the numbers each option changes**: a dry-run over the last 3 days (E.6), as Sid's members saw the tax and GovSim agents saw stock.
- **Measure what the rule was meant to change**, not the vote: trees standing, animals per pen, deposit share, light level (lesson 138).
- **Expect free-riding on levy topics** and decide before the run whether sanctions (topic 12) are on the ballot, since GovSim-SelfGovern's voters with extended thinking passed 25% of exile proposals against 2% without it.

## Not worth it here

- **PvP, griefing and mob toggles** (Towny's `pvp`, `mobs`, `fire`, `explosion`): the world is peaceful, bots never grief, and protection is already in code.
- **Wars, alliances and diplomacy** (Mount Augusta's two-thirds rule for war): one village, no rivals.
- **Mob votes or other one-off content picks**: one decision, no recurrence, and losers vanish.
- **Villager trading halls, lag machines, redstone farms**: no villagers or redstone in agents' skills; the one useful lag rule (herd caps) is topic 6.
- **Prices, currency and shop permits** (Hermitcraft's diamonds and permits, Towny taxes in money): agents do not trade with each other; revisit if personal chests lead to exchange.
- **Exile or banishment** (GovSim-SelfGovern's decisive lever): nobody starves here, and removing an agent breaks the experiment's fixed population; keep sanctions graduated.
- **Export bans** (Dwarf Fortress): no trade partner to export to.
- **Frostpunk-style moral laws** (child labour, organ transplants, burial): no analogue in a peaceful world without damage or death.
- **Religion by ballot**: Sid's religion spread by talk, not votes; it belongs in E.7's culture paths as a norm or saying, not a code-enforced rule.
- **Free-form constitutions written by agents** (Sid's Google Doc, DemocracyCraft bills): hard to enforce, invite invented rules (the AI Village's tie-break), and cost talk (MineCollab); typed rules with numbers do the same work.
